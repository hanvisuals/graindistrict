## 1. What we are building

Grain District is a creator-focused YouTube pre-production studio. The intended flow
is not just “ask AI for a script.” It should turn a rough idea into a filmable video:

1. The creator enters an idea or asks the Idea Studio for directions.
2. The creator chooses the approximate video duration near the beginning.
3. Grain District summarizes the project direction in three decisions.
4. It creates a chaptered, editable voiceover with a real narrative arc.
5. The creator can revise the voiceover before entering the visual grid.
6. B-roll, shot planning, production notes, and the visual board are derived from the
   final accepted voiceover rather than from an obsolete first draft.

The visual language is dark, cinematic, restrained, and editorial. The user likes the
existing Idea Studio especially; its three differentiated directions feel genuinely
useful. The script is the most important part of the product.

## 2. Product and editorial decisions made with the user

### Duration belongs at the start

- The user must be able to choose an approximate target from 1–20 minutes with a
  draggable control.
- A selected duration is a production constraint, not decorative metadata.
- If the user chooses 6–8 or 9 minutes, the delivered voiceover must contain enough
  spoken material for that range. A short paragraph is not an acceptable result.
- The implementation now enforces the selected voiceover runtime and repairs or
  rejects materially short output.

### The voiceover must feel lived-in, not like an abstract essay generator

The user rejected scripts made of disconnected poetic observations. The desired voice
is natural and “from inside” the experience: concrete actions, ordinary details,
specific decisions, and emotional subtext. It may remain poetic, but poetry cannot
replace context or causality.

An alarm/routine opening was discussed only as an example of an ordinary, natural
entry into a story. It is not a template and the system must not put an alarm into
every video.

A strong story-led script generally needs:

- a concrete reason this video begins now;
- an initiating situation, choice, constraint, or unanswered question;
- visible attempts and resistance;
- discoveries that change the narrator’s understanding or next action;
- escalation or a real knot in the middle;
- an earned answer, result, or narrowed understanding;
- a final physical action or image that carries the meaning without reopening the
  whole story.

The chapters should be generated by the system, but chapter labels alone do not create
structure. Every chapter needs an entry state, action, complication, and changed exit
state. The next chapter should exist because something changed in the previous one.

This method must adapt by genre:

- experiential/process stories: motivation → binding rule/attempt → resistance →
  discovery → earned result → final action;
- investigation/mystery: exact question → named clues → contradiction → evidence-led
  answer → final image;
- tutorials/guides: decision problem → explicit criteria → promised items/options →
  limits/trade-offs → selection map;
- visual essays: concrete observation → claim → counterexample → narrower synthesis →
  final image;
- factual documentaries: verified context and observable actions; unfilmed outcomes
  stay prospective;
- fictional/dramatized videos: invention is allowed, but chronology, evidence, point
  of view, and physical continuity still have to make sense.

### Fiction and dramatization are allowed

The user explicitly said not everything in a video has to be literally real; a project
can be fictionalized or staged if that makes the video stronger. The product therefore
distinguishes `storyReality` (factual, fictional, dramatized) from production state and
narrator time.

Important boundary: this permission does not justify silently fabricating factual
biography, research, dates, statistics, or personal memories and presenting them as
truth. Fiction should be treated as fiction/dramatization and kept internally coherent.

### Project Direction should be useful, not bureaucratic

The three cards should express a useful motivation/question/unfolding. The screen once
showed “No motivation was supplied by the creator; none is invented here.” The user
correctly considered this lifeless and useless. When a dramatized premise is allowed,
the system should propose a compelling narrative motivation rather than exposing an
internal safety refusal as creative direction.

The user selected the middle Idea Studio concept “I Followed One Rule for Thirty Days
and Kept Notes.” That direction later inspired the live phone-box experiment test.

### Voiceover revisions must refresh B-roll dependencies

The original bug was visible in a New York example: the creator revised a voiceover
line, entered the grid, and the B-roll still described films/locations from the old
line. This made the voiceover and visual plan unrelated.

The accepted rule is:

- the final accepted voiceover is the sole text source for B-roll generation;
- manual or AI-assisted line edits mark only the affected time range stale;
- entering/building the visual board first rebuilds those stale ranges;
- obsolete B-roll is removed;
- unaffected neighboring B-roll is preserved;
- a structural guard rejects a rebuilt timeline that visually skips a concrete beat
  from the revised voiceover.

### Script Studio should ultimately edit like a document

The user wants to read and edit the script as easily as a Word document, including
adding personal lines directly. The current Script Studio supports an editable script,
chapter reader, text selection, and targeted AI tools, but a fully polished Word-like
editing experience remains a product follow-up. Do not forget this requirement.

### Research Check panel was removed from Script Studio

The user found the large “Research Check” dashboard functionally distracting,
especially for non-factual topics. It occupied attention without improving the core
writing experience. Commit `1a3529a` removed that dashboard from Script Studio. The
underlying truth/evidence safeguards still exist for factual work; they should be
progressively disclosed rather than dominate every script.

## 3. Problems encountered and how they were addressed

The long-form generation pipeline originally tried to fail closed at too many stages.
Users repeatedly lost progress or were told to regenerate everything. Observed errors
included:

- `CHAPTER_UNDERWRITTEN (Bir Adım Geri)`;
- “Grain District protected your biography, but the contextual rewrite failed”;
- “The automatic chapter repair did not return a usable section”;
- “Could not rebuild every B-roll dependency from the final voiceover”;
- a 120-second whole-script editorial rewrite timeout;
- an unrelated transient `D1_ERROR: internal error` during sign-in, after which the
  user signed in successfully.

The recovery policy is now:

- preserve the last biography/safety-reviewed draft as `recoverableDraft`;
- never send the creator back to intake merely because a downstream polish step fails;
- patch unsafe biography lines in place where possible;
- quarantine only unsafe lines and route their chapters to local repair if a contextual
  rewrite fails;
- replace underwritten or invalid chapters locally instead of discarding the script;
- treat ordinary under-writing as advisory if a safe, complete-enough draft can open;
- if repair cannot finish, open the safe draft in Script Studio with an advisory;
- autosave the recovered draft;
- for clean non-factual videos longer than four minutes, skip the redundant full-script
  rewrite that repeatedly hit the 120-second timeout;
- still run deterministic quality/editorial checks and targeted repair;
- retain the full safety/editorial route for factual pre-shoot work where it matters.

## 4. Narrative and integrity safeguards implemented

The current code contains deterministic checks and prompt constraints for:

- chapter coverage and causal state change;
- exact repeated voiceover;
- chapter-boundary action restarts;
- unresolved contradictions and reopened ending questions;
- first-person point-of-view drift into external stage direction;
- internal safety/placeholder language leaking into published voiceover;
- fictional mysteries that never answer their exact question;
- generic or unnamed investigation clues;
- chronologies that move backward;
- the same physical clue being “discovered” twice;
- handwriting/handedness being treated as sufficient proof of identity;
- named age, date, and elapsed-duration contradictions;
- an exclusive/private-source opening contradicting a shared/public-source ending;
- an incomplete final locked physical action;
- a narrator claiming continuous clear observation while an object arrives unseen,
  unless a precise obstruction, lapse, alternate route, or equipment failure is named;
- premature conclusions: identity, memorial, cause, or motive remains a hypothesis
  until evidence earns the conclusion;
- factual pre-shoot scripts claiming outcomes that have not happened yet.

The impossible-observation guard was added after reading the live paper-plane output:
the narrator supposedly watched the bench continuously, yet a paper plane appeared
without being seen. That real QA finding became an explicit test and validation rule.

## 10. Natural continuation point

The core generation stage is much closer to the intended product and is no longer
trapped in repeated destructive repair failures. The next natural work is quality
validation rather than another broad rewrite:

1. Generate several genuinely different projects on current live `main`: at least one
   experiential video, one investigation, one practical guide, and one factual or
   pre-shoot documentary.
2. Read every script as a viewer, not merely as a validator. Check motivation, chapter
   state change, escalation, payoff, tone, and runtime.
3. Revise at least one voiceover line in Script Studio, enter the grid, and verify the
   B-roll now follows the final line and preserves unrelated ranges.
4. Record any remaining quality failure as a minimal reproducible case and add a
   regression test before changing prompts/validators.
5. After narrative reliability is demonstrated across genres, improve the Word-like
   direct editing experience.

Avoid endlessly adding generic prompt prose. Prefer a concrete failing example, the
smallest invariant that rejects or repairs it, and a representative live test.

## 11. August 15 pre-shoot comparison hardening

A six-minute live practical-guide test about recording interview audio with a phone
exposed three remaining failures: the supplied failed-interview motivation was replaced
with generic copy, unfilmed results were written as if data and a winner already existed,
and the script declared distance fixed before changing distance/microphone position.

The production code now:

- locks creator-supplied factual pre-shoot motivation through Creative Contract and
  Narrative Plan normalization, and verifies that the opening uses its distinctive
  factual anchors;
- rejects additional unfilmed-result language such as data already being on screen, a
  visible best candidate, an audience having heard the difference, or a result table
  already proving the answer;
- detects fixed-then-changed controls and multi-factor setups falsely described as
  single-variable tests;
- tells Creative Contract, chapter planning, chapter repair and final editorial passes
  to distinguish a single-variable test from a practical configuration comparison;
- preserves the concrete supplied motivation in the deterministic offline fallback.

Focused regression coverage is in `tests/testpreshotintegrity.mjs`. It passed together
with narrative chapters, Creative Contract v2, B-roll dependency refresh and narrative
closure suites.

## 12. August 16 live coffee test and prospective-process hardening

A fresh six-minute live project compared one coffee bean at three water temperatures.
The direction screen correctly preserved the supplied motivation and locked every
variable except temperature, but the generated voiceover exposed a second pre-shoot
failure class:

- future controls were rewritten as historical evidence (for example, the scale,
  grinder and timer supposedly had already stayed identical every morning);
- the unperformed test was narrated in the live present (the first cup was ready,
  brewing was under way, the table was filled and the tasting had finished).

The production validator and prompts now reject both behaviors. A planned control may
not become an invented fact about the earlier trigger, and no central test action,
prepared sample, filled result table or completed tasting may be narrated before filming.
The exact coffee failure is covered in `tests/testpreshotintegrity.mjs`.

A new live project in a different domain must be generated on the resulting deployed
commit. It should confirm that the opening retains the supplied real-world trigger,
all test actions remain prospective, the result remains unknown, and the ending names
what will be observed rather than pretending the comparison has already happened.
